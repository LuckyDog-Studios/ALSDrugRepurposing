STEP 4 — DISEASE IDENTIFICATION
================================================================================


CANDIDATE DISEASE: Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)
SUBTYPE / ASPECT:  Neuroinflammatory and multi-system proteomic signature
                   (likely from CSF and/or plasma proteomics of ALS patients)

CONFIDENCE: Medium-High

REASONING
---------
The combined protein landscape of both networks creates a highly specific
multi-system signature that most closely matches ALS:

1. NEUROINFLAMMATION & COMPLEMENT ACTIVATION (strongest signal)
   - Primary network: CFHR1, CFHR2, CFHR3, CFHR4, CFHR5, CR2, CR1L, MBL2,
     C3, C1S, C5AR2, VSIG4, ITGAM — an unusually dense cluster of complement
     regulators and receptors.
   - Extended network: CFB, CFI, C3, C1S — alternative pathway activation.
   - Complement activation is a well-documented pathological feature of ALS,
     with C3, C1q, and CFHR proteins elevated in ALS CSF/plasma.
   - ITGAM (CD11b) and CD163 (M2 macrophage marker) point to microglia/
     macrophage-mediated neuroinflammation — central to ALS pathology.

2. OXIDATIVE STRESS & NADPH OXIDASE (strong signal)
   - Primary: CYBB (NOX2), NOXA1, NOXO1, NOX3, MPO — the full NADPH oxidase
     complex is represented, indicating active ROS generation.
   - Extended: GCLM, GGT5, GPX3, GSTM1 — antioxidant response (glutathione
     pathway) engaged as a counter-response.
   - Oxidative stress through NOX2/ROS is a hallmark of ALS motor neuron
     degeneration; SOD1-ALS models show this prominently.

3. MITOCHONDRIAL DYSFUNCTION (strong signal)
   - Extended: NDUFA12 (Complex I), COX5A (Complex IV), FECH, ISCU, FOXRED1,
     HSPD1 (HSP60), MRPL37, MRPL19.
   - Mitochondrial dysfunction is one of the earliest and most consistent
     pathological findings in ALS motor neurons.

4. NEUROLOGICAL / CNS-SPECIFIC PROTEINS
   - MBP (Myelin Basic Protein): present in ALS CSF due to oligodendrocyte/
     myelin pathology around motor axons.
   - NCAM1: neural adhesion; altered in ALS.
   - DBN1 (Drebrin): neuronal actin-binding protein, reduced in ALS and AD.
   - ABAT + ALDH5A1: GABA catabolism; inhibitory interneuron loss in ALS
     drives GABAergic imbalance.

5. ACUTE PHASE RESPONSE
   - Primary: A2M, AHSG, HP, HPX, ORM1, ORM2, SERPINA3, ITIH4, TTR, CP.
   - This large cluster of positive and negative acute phase proteins reflects
     systemic inflammation, consistent with ALS blood/plasma profiles.
   - TTR (transthyretin) is reduced in ALS CSF — it may be included as a
     network seed.

6. LIPID / CHOLESTEROL DYSREGULATION
   - ABCA1, OSBPL9/10/11, FDFT1, HMGCS1 — cholesterol metabolism is disrupted
     in ALS; ABCA1-mediated cholesterol efflux in glia is altered.

7. NK / CYTOTOXIC IMMUNE CELLS
   - KIR2DL3, KIR3DL2, KIR3DL3, KLRC3-2, CD8B2, PTPRC, LCP2 — elevated NK
     and cytotoxic T cell activity has been observed in ALS patient blood.

WHY NOT OTHER DISEASES
----------------------
- Multiple Sclerosis: MBP is the key MS autoantigen, but MS lacks the
  CFHR1-5 cluster, the NADPH oxidase complex, and the GABA/mitochondrial
  signature seen here. MS networks would show more adaptive immune/Th17 genes.
- Alzheimer's Disease: CFHR1-5 and A2M appear in AD too, and DBN1 is reduced
  in AD. However, AD networks lack the NADPH oxidase cluster (NOXA1/NOXO1),
  the GABA enzyme pair (ABAT/ALDH5A1), and the MBP-NCAM1 motor neuron pattern.
- Parkinson's Disease: PD networks typically show alpha-synuclein, LRRK2,
  Parkin/PINK1 pathway — none present here.

TOP 5 MOST DIAGNOSTIC PROTEINS
-------------------------------
1. MBP    — Myelin Basic Protein: motor neuron/oligodendrocyte pathology;
            elevated in ALS CSF, not a typical plasma protein
2. CFHR1-5 — Complete cluster of complement H-related proteins; linked to
            dysregulation of alternative complement in ALS neuroinflammation
3. CYBB + NOXA1 + NOXO1 + NOX3 — Full NADPH oxidase assembly; ROS-mediated
            motor neuron death is a defining ALS mechanism
4. ABAT   — GABA aminotransferase; inhibitory interneuron/GABAergic dysfunction
            is documented in ALS spinal cord and cortex
5. NDUFA12 + COX5A + FOXRED1 — Mitochondrial Complex I/IV; bioenergetic
            failure in motor neurons is an early ALS hallmark

ALTERNATIVE CANDIDATES (lower confidence)
------------------------------------------
- Hereditary Transthyretin Amyloidosis (hATTR): TTR present + amyloid link,
  but hATTR networks wouldn't have CFHR cluster or GABA enzymes.
- Age-related Macular Degeneration (AMD): CFHR1-5 are the canonical AMD risk
  genes, but AMD lacks the neurological/GABA/mitochondrial features.
- ALS + FTD overlap: possible, given the neuroinflammatory and cognitive
  proteome overlap, but motor neuron proteins are more prominent.

SUMMARY
-------
The two networks together describe a disease with:
  (a) heavy complement/neuroinflammatory activation in both blood and CNS
  (b) oxidative stress (NOX complex) driving cell death
  (c) mitochondrial energy failure
  (d) GABA inhibitory circuit dysfunction
  (e) myelin/motor axon pathology (MBP, NCAM1)
  (f) systemic acute phase response

This multi-system profile — motor neuron + neuroinflammation + mitochondria +
oxidative stress + GABA — is most consistent with ALS (Amyotrophic Lateral
Sclerosis), likely reflecting proteomics data from two biological compartments
(e.g., primary = blood/plasma, extended = CSF or spinal cord tissue).
